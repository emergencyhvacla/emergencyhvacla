Common causes

Common Reasons for Furnace Emergencies

Most emergency calls come from a few causes. Whether the cause is serious depends on the assessment.

Safety first. If you smell gas, hear a hissing sound, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the home. Call 911 or SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 from outside. Do not operate light switches, and do not try to troubleshoot gas equipment yourself.

Ignition failure

The furnace tries to light but cannot, leaving the home without heat.

Blower failure

Heat is made but not delivered, and the furnace may overheat.

Safety lockout

The furnace shuts down to protect itself until the cause is fixed.

Electrical fault

Tripping breakers or a failed control board stops the furnace.

Gas or combustion concern

These are safety situations that need immediate attention.

Aging equipment

Old furnaces fail more often, often on the coldest nights.

These are common possibilities, not a diagnosis. The furnace needs an assessment to confirm what is wrong.

Before you call

Furnace Emergency? 5 Safe Things to Do First

Safety first. If none of these apply and you still have no heat, call.

  1. Smell gas or hear hissing? Leave. Call 911 or SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 from outside. Do not touch switches.
  2. CO alarm sounding? Leave. Get everyone outside and call 911.
  3. Check the thermostat. Heat mode, setpoint above room temperature, fresh batteries.
  4. Check the filter and furnace switch. Replace a clogged filter and make sure the switch is on.
  5. Check the breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call.

Do not open or repair gas equipment yourself

Furnaces involve combustion, gas, high voltage, and moving parts. In an emergency, leave the repair to a qualified technician.

  • Have your address and cross streets ready
  • Describe what the furnace is doing and when it started
  • Mention any gas smell, noise, or alarm
  • Know the furnace type and approximate age, if you can

Repair vs. replacement

Repair or Replace During an Emergency?

A cold night is a poor time to decide on a new furnace, but it is worth thinking about. Many homeowners get the home warm first and decide afterward.

A common rule of thumb multiplies the furnace's age in years by the repair estimate. If the total is above $5,000, price a replacement. It is a guide, not a quote.

Be an informed homeowner

What to Ask Before You Approve Any Furnace Repair

Whichever company you choose, these questions help you understand the work and the cost before you agree to it.

What exactly failed?

Ask which part or control is the problem and why it caused your symptom. A clear explanation is a good sign.

Can I see it?

Ask to be shown the failed part or reading, especially for large findings such as a cracked heat exchanger.

Is there a written estimate?

Ask for the scope and total in writing before work begins, and what happens if the scope changes.

Is the diagnostic fee credited?

Many companies credit a diagnostic fee toward the repair, but not all do. Ask before the visit.

Is the contractor licensed?

HVAC work in California requires a CSLB C-20 license. Ask for the number and check it at cslb.ca.gov.

Does this fix the cause?

Ask whether the repair addresses the root cause or only the symptom, so the problem does not return.
