How to Change a Furnace Filter in Los Angeles

A clean filter is the simplest, cheapest thing you can do for your furnace. This guide shows how to find it, pick the right size, and change it in about five minutes — no tools needed for most homes.

Quick answer

How Often Should I Change My Furnace Filter?

Check your furnace filter every month and change it every 1 to 3 months. In Los Angeles, homes with pets, nearby construction dust, or a system that runs often should check monthly. A grey, clogged filter strains the blower, weakens airflow, and can lead to overheating and short cycling.

Step by step

How to Change the Filter

Turn the thermostat to off and, if your furnace has an accessible power switch nearby, turn that off too. The filter sits in a slot on the furnace cabinet or in the return-air grille — usually a rectangular slot with a cover or a small door.

  1. Pull the old filter out and note the size printed on its frame, for example 16x25x1.
  2. Note the arrow on the frame. It shows the direction air flows, and it should point toward the furnace, not away from it.
  3. Slide the new filter in with the arrow pointing the same way.
  4. Close the cover, turn the power and thermostat back on, and write the date on the filter frame so you remember when it was changed.

If the slot is inside the furnace cabinet behind a screwed panel, or you cannot find the filter at all, leave the panel in place and ask a professional — furnace cleaning visits include checking the filter and airflow.

Choosing a filter

Which Filter Should I Buy?

Buy the exact size printed on the old filter. For most Los Angeles homes, a standard pleated filter (MERV 8 to 11) is a good balance: it catches dust and pet hair without choking airflow. A very thick or very high-MERV filter can restrict airflow on older systems, so do not go above what your furnace manual allows. If you have allergies, change the filter more often rather than buying the densest filter available.

Why it matters

What a Dirty Filter Does to Your Furnace

A clogged filter forces the blower to work harder, heats the furnace up, and can trip a safety limit that shuts the system off — which is why a dirty filter is one of the most common causes of a furnace that keeps turning on and off. It also lets dust reach the blower and burners, which is what a yearly furnace cleaning addresses. Five minutes with a filter is far cheaper than a blower motor repair.

Stop and call a professional if…

Do not open the furnace cabinet, touch burners, the flame sensor, or wiring. If the furnace makes a loud banging noise, smells of gas, or will not restart after a filter change, call (213) 771-9223.

If you smell gas at any point, leave the home immediately and call 911 or SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 from outside. Do not use light switches or try to fix a gas issue yourself.

FAQs

Furnace Filter Questions

How often should I change my furnace filter in Los Angeles?

Check it every month and change it every 1 to 3 months. Homes with pets, dust from remodeling, or a system that runs daily should lean toward monthly changes.

What size filter does my furnace need?

The size is printed on the frame of the old filter, for example 16x25x1. Buy that exact size. A filter that is too small lets dust slip around the edges.

Which way does the arrow on the filter point?

The arrow shows airflow direction and should point toward the furnace, not away from it. If you are unsure, the arrow usually points toward the blower compartment.

Can a dirty filter cause my furnace to stop working?

Yes. A clogged filter restricts airflow, can overheat the furnace, and may trip a safety limit that shuts it off. It is also a common cause of short cycling.
