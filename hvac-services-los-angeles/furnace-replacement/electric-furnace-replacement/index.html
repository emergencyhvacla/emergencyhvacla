What changes the price

What Drives the Price

Heater size in kW

Units run from about 5 kW to 20 kW or more. Mild winters often need less heat.

Breaker and wire

A larger unit needs a bigger breaker and thicker wire. Old wiring may need work.

Panel capacity

Your panel must handle the load. A panel upgrade adds cost.

Air handler match

The unit often shares a cabinet with the AC coil. Parts must match.

Duct condition

Leaky or undersized ducts waste heat and strain the blower.

Location

Closet, attic, and crawl space installs have different access needs.

Before you replace

Common Problems With Electric Heat

Some failures are cheap to fix. Ask for a repair price first on a newer unit.

Failed heating element

Elements wear out and can be replaced on many units.

Bad sequencer or relay

These control the heat stages. They are common repair parts.

Tripped breaker

Reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call.

Weak blower

A tired blower motor cuts airflow and heat.

Dirty filter

A clogged filter can trip the high limit switch.

Age over 20 years

Older units may be worth replacing instead of repairing.

Los Angeles homeowner guide

Los Angeles Rules for Electric Heat

Permits in Los Angeles

Replacing HVAC equipment in the City of Los Angeles generally needs a permit. Ask who pulls it and who books the inspection.

Title 24 energy code

The 2025 Energy Code applies to permits filed on or after January 1, 2026. Duct leak testing and third-party checks may apply.

LADWP heat pump rebates

LADWP has published heat pump HVAC rebates that rose for installs on or after November 1, 2025. Amounts and rules change, so confirm them first.

Know your electric rate

Los Angeles rates vary by utility and plan. Ask for a running cost estimate before you choose.

Check incentives first

State and utility programs change often. TECH Clean California single-family incentives have been closed or waitlisted. The federal 25C credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.

Check the contractor license

California HVAC work needs a CSLB C-20 license. Ask for the number and look it up at cslb.ca.gov before you sign.

What to expect

How an Electric Swap Works

  1. Check the panel. A technician confirms breaker and panel capacity.
  2. Pick the heater size. Size comes from a load calculation in kW.
  3. Match the air handler. The cabinet, coil, and blower must fit together.
  4. Pull the permit. Your contractor files the permit with the city.
  5. Install and wire. The unit is set, wired, and connected to ducts.
  6. Test and inspect. Heat stages, airflow, and safety controls are checked.

Helpful answers

Electric Heat Questions

Short answers to common questions from Los Angeles homeowners.

Are electric furnaces cheaper than gas?

They cost less to buy and install. They usually cost more to run, because electricity costs more per unit of heat than gas in most areas.

How long do electric furnaces last?

Published guides say 20 to 30 years. Heating elements and sequencers may fail sooner. Both can be replaced.

Does electric heat need a special breaker?

Yes. Most need a dedicated high-amp breaker and heavy wire sized to the unit. A technician checks your panel capacity first.

What heater size do I need?

Size comes from a load calculation. Los Angeles homes often need less heat than homes in cold states. A unit that is too large wastes money.

Is a heat pump better than electric resistance heat?

A heat pump moves heat instead of making it, so it often uses far less power. It costs more upfront. Compare quotes and LADWP rebates.

Can I swap my gas furnace for an electric one?

Yes, if your panel and wiring can handle the load. The gas line must be capped safely and a permit applies. Ask for a home review first.

How long does an electric install take?

Often one day. Electrical upgrades or duct changes add time.

How much does electric heat cost to run in Los Angeles?

It depends on your utility, rate plan, and heat use. Ask your installer for an estimate based on unit size and your rate.
