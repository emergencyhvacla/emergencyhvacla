Common causes

Why Furnace Thermostats Stop Working

The thermostat is often blamed first, but it is only one link in the chain. These are the common causes on either side of it.

Safety first. If you smell gas, hear a hissing sound, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the home. Call 911 or SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 from outside. Do not operate light switches, and do not try to troubleshoot gas equipment yourself.

Dead batteries

Battery-powered models stop working when batteries run low, sometimes with little warning.

Loose or damaged wiring

Loose terminals or damaged cable can break the signal.

No steady power

Smart models need a C-wire or power adapter. Without it they may reset or lose connection.

Wrong settings

Heat mode, fan setting, schedule, and hold settings can all prevent the heat you expect.

Poor location

A thermostat near a sunny window, vent, or kitchen reads the wrong temperature.

Furnace-side fault

The furnace may not respond to a good signal because of a control, safety, or ignition problem.

These are common possibilities, not a diagnosis. The furnace needs an assessment to confirm what is wrong.

Before you call

Thermostat Problem? 5 Safe Things to Check First

These checks need no tools and no opening of the furnace. If the problem remains, call.

  1. Check mode and setpoint. Confirm Heat mode and a setpoint a few degrees above room temperature.
  2. Replace the batteries. Use fresh batteries if your thermostat takes them.
  3. Check the furnace switch and breaker once. A tripped breaker or off switch can blank the thermostat. If the breaker trips again, stop and call.
  4. Check the schedule and hold. A program or hold can override the setting you expect.
  5. Smell gas? Leave the home. Call 911 or SoCalGas from outside. Do not touch switches.

Do not rewire a thermostat on a live system

Thermostat wiring is low voltage, but the furnace behind it is not. Wrong wiring can damage the control board. Switch off furnace power before touching any wire, or call a professional.

  • Note what the thermostat display shows
  • Note the thermostat brand and whether it is smart or basic
  • Note when the problem started
  • Photograph the wiring before anyone disconnects it

Be an informed homeowner

What to Ask Before You Approve Any Furnace Repair

Whichever company you choose, these questions help you understand the work and the cost before you agree to it.

What exactly failed?

Ask which part or control is the problem and why it caused your symptom. A clear explanation is a good sign.

Can I see it?

Ask to be shown the failed part or reading, especially for large findings such as a cracked heat exchanger.

Is there a written estimate?

Ask for the scope and total in writing before work begins, and what happens if the scope changes.

Is the diagnostic fee credited?

Many companies credit a diagnostic fee toward the repair, but not all do. Ask before the visit.

Is the contractor licensed?

HVAC work in California requires a CSLB C-20 license. Ask for the number and check it at cslb.ca.gov.

Does this fix the cause?

Ask whether the repair addresses the root cause or only the symptom, so the problem does not return.

Helpful answers

Furnace Thermostat Repair Los Angeles: FAQs

Short answers to common questions. They are general information, not a diagnosis of your furnace.

Why is my furnace not responding to the thermostat?

Common causes include wrong mode or settings, dead batteries, loose wiring, or a furnace-side fault such as a safety or control issue. Check mode, setpoint, and batteries first. If the furnace still does not respond, request an assessment.

Why is my thermostat blank?

A blank display usually means dead batteries or no power. Check the batteries, the furnace switch, and the breaker once. If it stays blank, call a technician.

How do I know if my thermostat or furnace is the problem?

If the thermostat shows a heat call and the furnace does nothing, the furnace or wiring is often involved. If the display is blank or settings reset, the thermostat or its power is more likely. An assessment confirms it.

How much does thermostat replacement cost?

Published national figures put professional installation at about $80 to $200 plus the thermostat, which can range from about $40 for a basic model upward. Wiring work adds cost. Ask for a written estimate.

Can I install a smart thermostat on my furnace?

Often yes, but compatibility depends on your wiring and furnace, and many need a C-wire. Check the maker's compatibility tool and ask a professional if unsure.

Why did my furnace stop working after I changed the thermostat?

Common causes are wrong wiring, no steady power, or compatibility problems. Switch the thermostat to Off and call if the furnace will not start.

Where should a thermostat be located?

Away from direct sun, vents, drafts, and kitchens, on an interior wall in a main living area. A poor location can make heating run wrong.
