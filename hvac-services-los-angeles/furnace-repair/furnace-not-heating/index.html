Common causes

Common Reasons a Furnace Stops Heating

A furnace that will not heat is usually a chain of small failures. These are the usual starting points.

Safety first. If you smell gas, hear a hissing sound, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the home. Call 911 or SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 from outside. Do not operate light switches, and do not try to troubleshoot gas equipment yourself.

Thermostat or fan setting

Fan set to On blows room-temperature air between heating cycles, which feels cold.

Dirty air filter

A clogged filter restricts airflow and can trigger an overheating shutdown.

Ignition or flame sensor problem

If the burners do not light or will not stay lit, the blower can run with no heat.

Safety shutdown

Overheating or flame problems can lock the furnace out until the cause is fixed.

Blocked vents or ducts

Closed registers or duct problems reduce warm air reaching rooms.

Aging equipment

Older furnaces lose capacity and fail more often.

These are common possibilities, not a diagnosis. The furnace needs an assessment to confirm what is wrong.

Before you call

Furnace Not Heating? 5 Safe Things to Check First

These checks need no tools and no opening of panels. If the furnace still does not heat, call.

  1. Check the thermostat. Set to Heat, setpoint above room temperature, fan on Auto, batteries fresh.
  2. Check the air filter. Replace it if it is dirty.
  3. Open vents and registers. Move furniture and open closed registers.
  4. Check the furnace switch and breaker once. If the breaker trips again, stop and call.
  5. Smell gas? Leave the home. Call 911 or SoCalGas from outside. Do not touch switches.

Do not keep restarting a furnace that shuts down

Safety shutdowns exist to protect your home. Repeated resets can hide an overheating or flame problem. Leave diagnosis and repair of gas equipment to professionals.

  • Note what the thermostat shows
  • Note whether the blower runs and whether the air is cool or warm
  • Note when it started and any sounds or smells
  • Know the furnace type and approximate age, if you can

Repair vs. replacement

Should I Repair or Replace a Furnace That Will Not Heat?

It depends on what failed and the furnace's condition. A small part on a healthy furnace is a repair. Safety findings or repeated failures on an old furnace are another matter.

A common rule of thumb multiplies the furnace's age in years by the repair estimate. If the total is above $5,000, price a replacement. It is a guide, not a quote.

Be an informed homeowner

What to Ask Before You Approve Any Furnace Repair

Whichever company you choose, these questions help you understand the work and the cost before you agree to it.

What exactly failed?

Ask which part or control is the problem and why it caused your symptom. A clear explanation is a good sign.

Can I see it?

Ask to be shown the failed part or reading, especially for large findings such as a cracked heat exchanger.

Is there a written estimate?

Ask for the scope and total in writing before work begins, and what happens if the scope changes.

Is the diagnostic fee credited?

Many companies credit a diagnostic fee toward the repair, but not all do. Ask before the visit.

Is the contractor licensed?

HVAC work in California requires a CSLB C-20 license. Ask for the number and check it at cslb.ca.gov.

Does this fix the cause?

Ask whether the repair addresses the root cause or only the symptom, so the problem does not return.

Furnace Not Heating Los Angeles: FAQs

Short answers to common questions. They are general information, not a diagnosis of your furnace.

Why is my furnace blowing cold air?

Often the thermostat fan is set to On, or the burners did not light or shut off for safety. Check that the fan is on Auto and the filter is clean. If the air stays cool, turn the furnace off and call a technician.

Why is my furnace running but not heating?

Common causes include a dirty filter, wrong thermostat settings, an ignition or flame sensor problem, or a safety shutdown from overheating. Check the thermostat and filter first. If the problem continues, request an assessment.

Can a dirty filter stop a furnace from heating?

Yes. A clogged filter restricts airflow and can cause the furnace to overheat and shut the burners off while the blower keeps running. Replace a dirty filter, then see whether heat returns.

Should I turn the furnace off if it is not heating?

If it is not heating after checking settings and the filter, turn it off and call, especially if you smell gas or burning. Do not keep restarting a furnace that shuts down.

What if I smell gas?

Leave the home immediately. Call 911 or SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 from outside. Do not use light switches or try to troubleshoot the furnace.

How much does it cost to fix a furnace that is not heating?

It depends on the cause. Published national figures put typical furnace repairs at about $150 to $600, with igniters around $150 to $300. Ask for a written estimate before work.

Why does my furnace heat for a few minutes and then stop?

This can point to a dirty flame sensor, restricted airflow, or an overheating safety shutdown. Replace a dirty filter, then request an assessment if it continues.
