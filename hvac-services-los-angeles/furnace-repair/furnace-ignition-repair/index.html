Common causes

Why Furnace Ignition Fails

Ignition parts work hard every heating cycle, and Los Angeles furnaces often sit idle for months before the first cold night. These are the usual suspects.

Safety first. If you smell gas, hear a hissing sound, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the home. Call 911 or SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 from outside. Do not operate light switches, and do not try to troubleshoot gas equipment yourself.

Worn or cracked igniter

Hot surface igniters are fragile and wear out with heat cycles. A cracked one may glow weakly or not at all.

Dirty flame sensor

Residue on the sensor can stop it from detecting the flame, so the furnace shuts the gas off after a few seconds.

Ignition control fault

The board that runs the start sequence can fail or lock out, even when the igniter and sensor are fine.

Airflow or venting restriction

A clogged filter, blocked vent, or draft problem can trigger a safety switch and stop the start-up.

Gas supply or valve problem

If gas does not reach the burners, a good igniter will not help. This needs qualified inspection.

Moisture or condensate issues

High-efficiency furnaces can stop starting if the condensate drain backs up and trips a safety switch.

These are common possibilities, not a diagnosis. The furnace needs an assessment to confirm what is wrong.

Before you call

Furnace Will Not Light? 5 Safe Things to Check First

These checks need no tools and no opening of panels. If the furnace still will not light, stop and call.

  1. Check the thermostat. Confirm it is set to heat, the setpoint is above room temperature, and the batteries are fresh.
  2. Check the filter. A clogged filter restricts airflow and can trip a safety switch. Replace it if dirty.
  3. Check the furnace switch and breaker. Make sure the wall switch near the furnace is on. If the breaker trips again after one reset, stop and call.
  4. Listen and watch. Note whether you hear clicking, see a glow, or hear the inducer fan. This helps narrow the cause.
  5. Smell gas? Leave the home. Call 911 or SoCalGas from outside. Do not touch switches.

Do not open or repair gas equipment yourself

Ignition parts sit next to gas, high voltage, and hot components. Resetting a furnace again and again can also hide a safety lockout.

  • Note what the furnace does when it tries to start
  • Note any flashing LED pattern on the control board
  • Note when the problem started
  • Know the furnace type and approximate age, if you can

Repair vs. replacement

Should I Repair or Replace a Furnace With Ignition Problems?

An igniter or flame sensor on a furnace in otherwise good shape is usually a repair decision. Repeated failures on an older furnace are a different case.

A common rule of thumb multiplies the furnace's age in years by the repair estimate. If the total is above $5,000, price a replacement. It is a guide, not a quote.

Helpful answers

Furnace Ignition Repair Los Angeles: FAQs

Short answers to common questions. They are general information, not a diagnosis of your furnace.

Why is my furnace clicking but not lighting?

Clicking usually means the furnace is trying to start but the ignition sequence is not completing. Causes include a failed igniter, an ignition control fault, or no gas reaching the burners. Turn the furnace off if it keeps cycling and call a technician.

What does a furnace igniter do?

The igniter lights the gas at the burners. Many modern furnaces use a hot surface igniter that glows hot enough to ignite the gas when the valve opens. If it fails, the furnace cannot light.

Why does my furnace light and then shut off?

This often points to a dirty or failing flame sensor, which confirms the flame is present. If the sensor cannot detect it, the furnace shuts the gas off for safety. Airflow and venting problems can cause similar behavior.

How much does furnace igniter replacement cost?

Published national figures put igniter replacement at about $150 to $300 including parts and labor. Your price depends on the furnace, access, and timing. Ask for a written estimate before work starts.

Can I replace a furnace igniter myself?

It is not recommended. The igniter is fragile, sits near gas and high voltage, and the wrong part or a missed fault can create a safety problem. Have a qualified technician diagnose and replace it.

How do I know whether it is the igniter or the flame sensor?

Symptoms help but do not confirm it. No glow suggests the igniter or control. Burners that light then shut off within seconds suggest the flame sensor. A technician needs to test the parts.

Is it safe to keep resetting my furnace?

No. Repeated resets can hide a safety lockout. If the furnace will not light after one reset, leave it off and call. If you smell gas, leave the home and call 911 or your gas utility.
