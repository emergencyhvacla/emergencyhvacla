Choose well

80% or 95% AFUE? What Changes

80% AFUE, non-condensing

Lower price. Vents through a metal flue. About 20 cents of each gas dollar leaves up the flue.

95% to 98% AFUE, condensing

Higher price. Vents through PVC and drains acidic condensate. Uses less gas per season.

Single, two-stage, or modulating

More stages give steadier heat and quieter runs. They also cost more.

Blower motor type

Variable speed blowers move air evenly and help cooling season too.

Furnace size

Los Angeles winters are mild. A unit that is too big cycles often and wears out sooner.

Install location

Attic, closet, garage, and crawl space installs have different clearance and venting needs.

Los Angeles homeowner guide

Los Angeles Rules for a New Gas Furnace

In 2025 the South Coast AQMD board voted against a plan to phase out new gas furnace sales. Rule 1111 still applies.

Low-NOx gas furnaces

South Coast AQMD Rule 1111 limits NOx from new gas furnaces under 175,000 Btu/h to 14 ng/J. Ask for the certified model number.

Permits in Los Angeles

Replacing HVAC equipment in the City of Los Angeles generally needs a permit. Ask who pulls it and who books the inspection.

Title 24 energy code

The 2025 Energy Code applies to permits filed on or after January 1, 2026. Duct leak testing and third-party checks may apply.

Wall and floor furnaces

Many older Los Angeles homes use wall or floor furnaces. These differ from central furnaces. Tell us which type you have.

Test carbon monoxide alarms

Check that alarms work on every level when you get a new furnace.

Check the contractor license

California HVAC work needs a CSLB C-20 license. Ask for the number and look it up at cslb.ca.gov before you sign.

What to expect

What Happens on Swap Day

  1. Gas and power go off. A technician shuts off both supplies to the old furnace.
  2. The old unit comes out. The technician disconnects venting and ducts and removes the furnace.
  3. The new furnace goes in. It is set, then connected to gas, vent, power, and ducts.
  4. Leak and safety checks. The technician checks gas joints, flame, venting, and safety controls.
  5. Airflow and duct checks. Title 24 may require duct leak testing and sealing.
  6. Inspection. A city inspector reviews the permitted work.

Gas Furnace Questions From Homeowners

Short answers to common questions from Los Angeles homeowners.

How much does a gas furnace cost to install in Los Angeles?

California homeowners usually pay about $4,500 to $9,000. A common middle is $6,500. The 80% AFUE models cost less than 95% condensing models. Get the model, size, and permit in writing.

What is the difference between 80% and 96% AFUE?

AFUE is the share of fuel that becomes heat. An 80% unit sends about 20% up the flue. A 96% unit sends about 4%. The 96% unit costs more and needs PVC venting.

Does a new gas furnace need to be low-NOx in Los Angeles?

Yes, in the South Coast air district. Rule 1111 caps NOx at 14 ng/J for new gas furnaces under 175,000 Btu/h. Ask for the certified model number.

Should I switch from gas to electric or a heat pump?

Gas often costs less to run than electric resistance heat. A heat pump can also work in mild winters. Compare quotes, utility rates, and rebates for your home.

How do I know if my heat exchanger is cracked?

Only a technician can confirm it. Warning signs include soot, a yellow flame, or a carbon monoxide alarm. Turn the furnace off and call if you see them.

Can I keep my old ducts with a new gas furnace?

Often yes, if they are in good shape. Title 24 may require leak testing and sealing. Leaky ducts waste heat and cooling.

How long does a gas furnace swap take?

Most like-for-like swaps take about one day. Venting changes or duct work add time. Confirm the schedule before work starts.

What should a gas furnace quote include?

Ask for the model number, AFUE, size in Btu/h, permit, venting, old unit disposal, and labor. Get it in writing before work starts.
